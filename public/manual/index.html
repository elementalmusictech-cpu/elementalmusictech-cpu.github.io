Your first pattern

Create a repeating phrase on a Tone track. Connect an instrument, place a few notes and hear them play back.

Connect an instrument

Prism has 16 tracks, with matching MIDI channels: Track 1 uses Channel 1, Track 2 uses Channel 2, and so on. For this example, route Track 1 to an instrument receiving Channel 1.

In an AUv3 host

  1. Add Prism as an AUv3 MIDI processor and add a receiving instrument.
  2. Connect Prism to the instrument using the host's MIDI routing. Filter the destination to Channel 1.
  3. Connect the instrument's audio output to your speakers or headphones.

In AUM, put Prism on a MIDI channel and the instrument on an Audio channel, then connect them in AUM's MIDI Routing matrix.

In the standalone app

Open MIDI routing at the top right. Expand Prism in the right-side source list, then connect its Channel 1 row to your instrument destination across the top. Set that instrument to receive Channel 1.

Start a Tone pattern

A new project opens on Track 1 with a choice of Tone Track or Drum Track. Choose Tone Track for pitched notes and chords.

To set up another track, tap the notification strip to open Track Select, choose an empty track, then press Seq to reach its Tone/Drum chooser.

Build a phrase

The keypad chooses notes; the step sequencer places them in time. Open the Notes keypad tab if another tool is showing.

  1. Tap a note on the keypad.
  2. Tap sequencer steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 to place trigs.
  3. Select another note and place a few more trigs.

Each trig stores the notes selected when you placed it.

Play and edit

Start the host's transport if using AUv3, then press Play in Prism. Tap a filled sequencer step to remove its trig or an empty step to add one. Hold a trig for Trig Focus to edit its stored notes. Press Stop when finished.

If you hear nothing, check the host transport, Channel 1 routing, the instrument's MIDI input and its audio output.

Save for later

Open the top-left Menu → Projects → Save Project. Enter a session name, choose a folder and tap SAVE SESSION.

In AUv3, also save the host session to retain its instrument and routing setup. The standalone folder shortcut also offers Save.

Interface and Control Buttons

Prism's workspace has four main areas: Control Bar, GUI, Keypad and Sequencer. The current state—such as Sequencer or FX—determines which tools they show.

The main interface

Area Purpose
1 Control Bar Transport and shared actions.
2 GUI Displays the current state (Seq, FX, CC, Song, etc.).
3 Keypad Notes, pads and contextual controls.
4 Sequencer Place and edit trigs; page and sequence controls below.
5 Top Bar (large screens) Tracks, Patterns, Sounds, Scenes, mute profiles and piano roll.

The four keypad tabs switch between note entry and editing tools. They are listed here from top to bottom; Notes is at the bottom.

Tap a tab to open it; tap it repeatedly to cycle its layers.

Tap, hold, double-tap and drag

These gestures work across most Prism controls.

Gesture Usual action
Tap Select a control or perform its visible action.
Hold Access a secondary action or temporary view. A small corner mark on the left side indicates a hold action.

Colors indicate scope: Global controls use Prism's global color, track controls use the track's color, and Drum pad controls use the pad's color.

The highlighted control shows the current selection or active option. For control-specific help, use Menu → Help & Support → Help Mode.

Menu and notifications

Use the top-left controls for projects, settings and track navigation.

The Prism-shaped Menu icon is at the top left. It opens Projects, Preferences and Help & Support. Tap the notification strip next to it to open Track Select. Choose a track and press Seq for its sequencer home.

Play, Stop and Follow

Playback controls stay available while you move between editing states.

  • Play starts playback. In AUv3, start the host transport first; N/A: Play Host means Prism is waiting for it.
  • Stop ends playback and recording and releases held notes. Hold Stop for Panic.
  • Pause, where available, holds the playback position. Standalone uses Play-to-restart instead.
  • Follow in Seq Setup keeps the visible sequence page aligned with playback. Moving pages manually pauses Follow; hold Play during playback to resume it.

In AUv3, tempo comes from the host. Standalone has its own BPM control.

Shift

Shift exposes secondary actions, including quick mutes and trig selection.

Hold Shift to show secondary controls temporarily, or tap Shift to toggle them on and off. With Shift on, you can mute or unmute:

Sequence trig Mute/unmute that trig.
Drum pad Mute/unmute the pad.
Tone lane Reveal the lanes; tap the active lane to toggle its mute.

Press Shift to show Sel. Trigs below the sequencer. Use its controls to select trigs for group editing.

Selections open Trig Focus for group edits.

Mute, mix and groove controls

Open Track Select to adjust the selected track, Tone lane or Drum pad. Mute toggles that part without deleting its trigs; Solo isolates it. Hold Solo to clear track solos (Solo Off For All).

Mixer shows track velocity offsets in the sequencer area and lane/pad offsets on the keypad; Delay offsets sequence timing. Hold Swing for groove templates or Human for Velocity, Length and Timing variation.

Groove offsets

Human weights are 0–100%. Local lane/pad Swing and Human offsets run −100 to +100% with 0 neutral. Local / Linked controls whether the selected context follows inherited settings.

Hold Mute to open MUTE OPTIONS, including Thru / PLocks, Thru / Melt, Mute / Melt and All Lanes / All Pads. Hold Mute again, or tap Mute while the options are open, to close them.

Copy and Paste

Copy and Paste work with the object currently under the gesture.

Hold Copy and tap the source, then hold Paste and tap the destination. Pasting a Drum pad or Tone lane replaces the destination's sequence.

You can copy: Trigs, Tone lanes, Drum pads, Arp patterns and steps, LFO settings, Scenes and automation lanes.

Capture

Capture provides shortcuts for storing the current editing state.

Hold Capture to reveal shortcuts for capturing the current state without opening its usual editor.

  • Capture + Seq: capture the current Pattern, then continue editing a copy.
  • Capture + Song: capture the current device state as a Song step.

Sequences and trigs

Every Tone lane and Drum pad has its own sequence. Select a lane or pad, then press Seq to open its sequencer steps.

Place trigs

Tap an empty step to place a trig, or a filled step to remove it. Tone trigs use the active keypad notes; Drum trigs use the selected pad.

To change an existing trig, hold its step to open Trig Focus. See Trig Focus and Step FX for editing stored notes and selecting multiple trigs.

Note input settings

Set these values before placing trigs. Each new trig uses the current input settings.

Control What it sets
Velocity How strongly the notes play.
Length How long the notes play.
Chance The probability a trig plays when eligible.
Every A repeating cycle that chooses which sequence loops the trig can play on.

Drag the input-note graphic vertically for Velocity or horizontally for Length. On Tone, tap an input note to edit it individually; tap again to edit all notes, or double-tap to remove it.

Every makes a trig conditional. For example, enable 1 of 2 visits to play on alternating loops. Hold Every to open its keypad: hold a position to set the cycle length, then tap positions to choose which visits play. Chance still applies to those visits.

More conditional options
  • Every cycles: Up to 16 positions. Tapping beyond the cycle extends it.
  • Tone lanes: Have separate Chance/Every conditions for the whole lane. Lane Chance choices are 12, 25, 37, 50, 62, 75, 87 and 100%.
  • Once: Only on the first sequence loop.
  • <: If the preceding trig played.
  • <<: If the last trig with conditional settings played.
  • !: If the preceding trig did not play.

To change these settings on a stored trig, use Trig Focus.

Set sequence length and resolution

Press Seq Setup below the sequencer to set how long the sequence runs and how its steps are spaced.

Res. Step spacing (such as 1/16 or 1/8), without moving stored trigs.
1 Page Adjusts resolution to show the entire sequence on one page.

Sequences can be up to 16 bars. For longer sequences, use the page controls below the trigs to view other steps.

Page shortcuts
Page control Action

Sequence setup options
Control Action
Legato Hold each trig until the next trig.

A shorter length retains trigs beyond its endpoint. Coarser resolution can hide off-grid trigs, marked by a corner triangle; use a finer grid to see them.

Hold Seq Setup for the temporary length keypad. Follow keeps the displayed page with playback. Auto-adjust Step Resolution in Preferences can keep a resized sequence on one page.

Open the transformation keypad tab to shift trigs or quantize their timing.

Quantize stored trigs

Open Quantize on the transformation keypad. You can also hold Seq Setup for its Quantize shortcut.

  1. Set Quant To for the timing grid and Amount for how strongly trigs move toward it.
  2. If needed, adjust the range with the piano-roll handles.
  3. Press Quantize to apply.
Quantize options
  • Amount: 0% leaves timing unchanged; 100% moves trigs to the nearest chosen grid point.
  • Range: Drag an end to resize, or the middle to move it; double-tap to reset to the full sequence.
  • Drum: Choose Pad or Track for the affected sequence(s).
  • Tone Trim: With a partial range selected, Trim removes trigs outside it.
  • Overlapping events: Trigs moved to the same pulse merge into one block.

Tone tracks

Tone tracks play pitched notes and chords using the Song scale. Each Tone track has four independent sequence lanes for layering different phrases.

Song key and scale

Song Key and Mode set the scale used by Tone keypads and scale-aware transformations. Open the main FX selector, then drag Song Key or Mode to change them. The settings apply to all Tone tracks.

Hold Song Key to open Key & Scale Engine. Choose a scale category on the left and a scale on the right; its notes appear in the keyboard preview. Press FX to return.

Custom scales and advanced choices
  • Scale families: Diatonic Modes, Minor Variants, Melodic Minor Modes, Pentatonic & Blues, Symmetric, Exotic / World and Bebop.
  • Chromatic: Choose Symmetric → Chromatic for all 12 pitches.
  • Custom pitches: With a custom scale selected, hold Song Key and use the pitch-class trigs to add or remove notes (at least one must remain).
  • Advanced controls: Hold Shift for Adv Key Config. Root-selection trigs set the root in ordinary modes; custom pitch trigs change the pitch collection in custom mode.

Tone note shifts follow scale steps, which aren't necessarily chromatic semitones. Imported Tone MIDI also follows the selected scale; the import browser lets you correct the detected source key before re-importing. See MIDI Import.

Tone keypad

The Tone keypad plays notes and selects pitches for new trigs. Bright pads show selected input notes; dim root markers help identify the scale.

Use the right octave control to change register. The central arrows move input notes by a scale step; hold an arrow to move an octave.

Latch (∞) holds notes after release; tap a latched note again to release it. Shift + Latch toggles keypad playback mute for silent note selection.

Live note input
  • Turning Latch off releases notes not physically held; notes still pressed release when you let go.
  • Keypad Touch Velocity in Preferences uses touch height to set input velocity (higher is louder).
  • Input Velocity also has a field, right-side control and note graphic. None of these rewrites stored trigs.

Input-note editing

The input-note display shows the notes that will be used for the next trig. By default, edits affect all input notes.

Tap one note cell to edit that note alone; tap it again to return to all notes. Double-tap a cell to remove it. Drag the input-note graphic horizontally for Length or vertically for Velocity; the pitch arrows move the selected note.

To change notes already in a trig, use Trig Focus.

Simple input chords

Three Chord slots build chords from any root on the Tone keypad. Each slot remembers its own richness and voicing, so you can switch between chord shapes while playing or entering trigs.

Select Chord 1, Chord 2 or Chord 3 on the Notes keypad, then play a root. The richness strip offers Root, Fifth, Triad, Seventh, Ninth and Eleventh. Hold the selected slot to edit its voicing.

Chord voicing controls
Control Effect
Sus Replaces the third with the fourth scale degree.
Open Widens a voicing by moving the next-to-top voice down an octave (with 3+ voices).
Walk Automatically chooses inversions/octaves for smoother movement between chords.
Bass Cycles Off → one octave below → two octaves below → Off.
▲ / ▼ Rotates inversions; disabled while Walk is on.

Root produces one note; inversions need at least two. Turning Walk off restores base inversion. Voicing changes affect new input, not trigs already stored.

Tone lanes

A Tone track has four independent sequence lanes. Use them to layer melodies, bass parts or chords with different rhythms; each lane stores its own trigs and sequence settings.

Tap Multi Seq (the 2 × 2 control), then tap a lane to edit it. For quick access, hold Shift on the normal keypad: tap a lane to select it, or tap the active lane to toggle its mute.

Hold Copy and tap a source lane; hold Paste and tap a destination lane to replace its stored phrase and settings.

Lane playback settings
  • Chance / Every: Control whether a whole lane plays on a loop, independently of individual trig conditions. Chance choices are 12, 25, 37, 50, 62, 75, 87, 100%. In Every, hold a position to set cycle length and tap positions to select eligible loops.
  • Chokes / Choked: A playing Chokes lane cuts off Choked lanes and suppresses their trigs on that pulse. Each lane can have one role: Off, Chokes or Choked; tap its active role again to clear it.
  • Arp: All four lanes share one Arp; each lane can enable or mute its own Arp participation.
  • All Lanes: Hold to clear trigs and reset all four lanes.

Tone piano roll

The piano roll shows stored notes by pitch and time. Open Seq to view the sequence's piano roll; on large layouts, the Top Bar also provides piano-roll access. Tap the roll to move to its corresponding sequence page.

Hold a roll trig to enter Trig Focus. In focus, tap notes to select them; Shift-tap adds to the selection. Tap empty time to release focus.

Piano-roll editing
Gesture Action
One-finger drag on selected trigs Horizontal changes time; vertical changes pitch.
One-finger drag away from selection Selects a range of trigs by time.
Two-finger drag horizontally Changes note length.
Two-finger drag vertically Changes velocity.

Outside focus, two-finger horizontal editing changes note lengths on the current page; vertical editing changes pitch. In focus, double-tap at the same horizontal position to release focus. Outside focus, double-tap resets the loop range. A finer grid or the piano roll reveals off-grid trigs.

Tone Lab

Tone Lab is the active lane's quick-edit panel for velocity, groove and length. Open it with the flask; use Vel Shift, Swing, Hmnz and Trig Steps, then tap the right arrow to return.

For generating or transforming stored rhythms, use Trig Tools.

Chord Blocks

Store a chord once and reuse it in trigs across tracks and patterns. Editing that block updates every trig that refers to it; each trig keeps its own timing and velocity.

Sharing and banks

Sharing and banks

Scope Remember
4 banks · A–D 8 blocks/bank · 8 notes/block
Project-wide Scene recalls the bank. Each trig keeps its timing/velocity.

Blocks are shared across tracks and patterns in the project. Editing one slot changes every trig using it. For a variation that should stay separate, copy the block to another slot first.

Each bank gives those eight slots another set of notes. Scenes recall the active bank, so the same slot number can provide different harmony in another Scene.

Open → edit → use

On a Tone track, open the music-note tab and reveal Chord Blocks.

1. Open

On the music-note tab, tap the two-square toggle and select Chord. Hold the cube to open the editor.

2. Edit

Choose Bank A–D, then hold a block and tap the right-hand note pads to change its notes.

Notes and empty blocks

Note pads follow the current scale and octave. Editing notes changes every trig using this block. An empty pattern can still have populated blocks. Selecting + may copy the current input notes into that slot. To start empty, select a slot you can change and tap trash.

3. Use

Tap the cube to return to the sequence. Choose a block preview, then tap empty sequence steps.

What is stored

Selecting a block sets input for future trigs. Existing steps keep their references; new trigs use the current input length and velocity.

Block tools

Block tools edit one shared chord slot; they do not copy or remove the trigs that reference it. To clear a block, select it and tap trash at the focused strip's right end.

A copied block is independent. Existing trigs keep their current references. Clearing or pasting changes a shared slot's contents; its trigs remain in the sequence.

Copy and note movement

Copy and note movement

  • Copy/Paste uses the block pads on the Chord Blocks keypad. Release Copy before holding Paste; pasting replaces the destination's notes.
  • Copying a block copies its notes, not its rhythmic uses in the sequence. Choose the copy as input to place new uses.
  • ▲ / ▼ moves notes in scale steps. Select a note in the focused strip to move that note; with none selected, the whole block moves.
Details

More gestures

  • Remove a sequence trig: tap its filled step. The shared block stays.
  • Lower companion trig: double-tap to clear a nonempty block; an empty block is randomized instead. In the randomization panel, it randomizes the corresponding block.
  • Return to ordinary notes: choose an ordinary note/chord input mode on the keypad.

Bank tools

Bank tools transform several blocks at once. Protected blocks can be excluded from the transformations. Tap dice left of the focused strip to reveal Randomize, Invert and Smooth; tap the return arrow to close.

Tap a block's padlock above its piano display in this panel to protect it from these transformations.

Bank transformations

These commands affect the active bank, so tracks and patterns using its blocks can change together. Lock the blocks you want to retain before applying a transformation.

Transformation details

Transformation details

Command Musical result
Randomize Generates note collections for eligible blocks, including empty slots.
Invert Reworks intervals and voicing within existing chords.
Smooth Looks for voicings with smoother note movement between blocks.

Block locks constrain these transformations. Clearing and Trigs to Blocks can still replace locked content.

Trigs to Blocks

Trigs to Blocks replaces matching stored chords with references to shared blocks; it changes the selected bank’s first slots. From the normal Tone sequence with Chord Blocks keypad active, hold Capture → press Trigs to Blocks → release both.

Prism confirms the conversion with [Converted Seq > Blocks].

Conversion rules

Conversion turns the active sequence's stored notes into block references. Repeated matching chords share a slot, while their sequence positions stay in place.

Remember Conversion does this
Whole sequence Overwrites the selected bank's first slots; other uses of those slots change too.
Up to 8 chords Extra distinct chords remain unconverted.
Note detail Uses the first note's length/velocity; separate per-note settings are lost.
Conversion details

Matching and overflow

  • Slots: creation begins at block 1 in the selected bank. Only created slots are replaced; unused slots keep their contents.
  • Matching: note count and order must match. Different note lengths or velocities do not create separate blocks.
  • Overflow: a ninth distinct chord stays as notes; a later repeat of one of the first eight can still become a reference.
  • Note detail: the first note supplies the reference's length and velocity. Separate settings for each chord note are not retained. This converts stored sequence notes; downstream FX are not rendered into blocks.

Drum tracks

Each Drum pad has its own sequencer, so kicks, snares and percussion can run different rhythms or lengths. Link their timing for a shared groove, or let independent pad lengths create polyrhythms.

Drum track setup

Choose Drum Track on an empty track to open Edit Drum Profile. Here you assign the notes your drum instrument expects.

Drum profile and MIDI output

Load an existing profile, or select a pad and set its name and MIDI Output under Edit Pad. Selecting a pad or changing MIDI Output auditions that note. Press Done to use the mapping on this track.

You can reopen the profile editor with Shift + wrench.

Pad Type

Pad Type identifies the pad's General MIDI (GM) drum type, such as Kick (36) or Snare (38). Prism uses it to match drum parts during MIDI import and generation. Choose a type from Edit Drum Profile → Pad Type, or select No Type. The pad's actual outgoing note is set separately by MIDI Output.

Saving and managing Drum profiles

  • Save: Updates the selected user profile. If the mapping is new, it creates a profile like As.
  • As: Saves a named copy; duplicate names receive a number. Editing a factory profile saves a user copy.
  • Star / cube: Favorite a profile / show or hide factory profiles. Favorite profiles are available as quick choices from the sequencer.
  • Blank top entry: Returns to an unnamed mapping.
  • Hold Del: Permanently deletes the selected library profile without confirmation.

Profile files can be backed up separately from projects and imported MIDI. See Files and saving.

Drum pad sequences

Tap a Drum pad to play it and select its sequencer. Tap empty sequencer steps to place trigs, or filled steps to remove them. Each pad retains its own trigs, input values and output mapping.

Multiple notes in one Drum sequence

Tap the Notes keypad tab again to open its second layer. Like Tone note input, this layer lets you select several MIDI notes for one trig—including notes associated with other pads or other MIDI note values. Those notes play from the selected pad's sequencer; switching input notes does not switch the sequence you're editing.

Input, mute and copy
  • Set Velocity, Length, Chance and Every before placing trigs. Hold a pad to sustain its input note.
  • The input Velocity slider, field or graphic adjusts input strength; Keypad Touch Velocity in Preferences also responds to touch height.
  • Holding the Notes tab opens Latch, keypad playback mute and touch-velocity settings. Latch holds notes until pressed again; playback mute selects pads silently.
  • Shift + pad toggles its sequence mute. Copy + source pad, then Paste + destination pad, replaces the destination's sequence while keeping its own MIDI Output mapping.

  • Hold a filled trig for Trig Focus, including Drum Velocity, Length, Ratchet/Roll and Flam Step FX.

Independent or shared timing

Drum pad sequences can have different lengths, creating evolving rhythms, or share one timeline so their beats stay aligned.

Open Seq Setup and tap Pad to link or unlink the selected pad. Linked pads share length, resolution, loop range, speed and page, while keeping their own trigs. Unlinked pads use independent timing. Hold Pad to apply the opposite of the selected pad’s link state to all pads.

Drum Lab

Drum Lab shapes individual pads and relationships between them. Open the flask, select a pad, then use its tools.

Tool What it does
Swap Exchanges two pads' stored rhythms, keeping each pad's MIDI output mapping.
Layer Adds selected pad output notes when the source pad's trig plays.
S-Chain Suppresses selected target pads' trigs when the source pad hits.
Mix Adjusts each pad's sequence velocity offset.

The top row also offers Vel Shift, Swing, Hmnz and Trig Steps for the active pad's strength, timing and sequence length. Tap the right arrow to return.

Drum Lab gestures and resets
  • Swap: Choose Swap, then the second pad. It exchanges sequence and Euclidean timing data; Prism selects that pad and shows Swapped Pads.
  • Layer: Tap target pads to add/remove their notes. Tap the source pad to clear its layers (RESET PAD LAYERS); vertical drag adjusts layer velocity offset. Targets can also play their own trigs.
  • S-Chain: Tap targets to toggle suppression; tap the source to clear them (RESET SIDECHAIN). Circular assignments are rejected with Sidechain N/A.
  • Mix: Drag vertically on a pad for its velocity offset. Vel Shift changes sequence playback velocity, not live input velocity.

  • Linked pads follow shared Trig Steps. Groove offsets remain local; playback speed and grid resolution are configured separately in Seq Setup.

Trig Focus and Step FX

Trig Focus edits notes and settings already stored in a trig. You can edit one trig or select several to change together, including their Step FX and CC locks.

Enter and lock Trig Focus

Hold a filled sequencer step to focus its trig. Release to exit, or enable the focus lock to keep editing after releasing your finger. With focus locked, tap the focused trig or highlighted Seq control to exit.

Slide upward from a held trig to retain focus temporarily, without enabling the persistent focus lock.

Select multiple trigs

Focus more than one trig to change their stored settings together. Press additional trigs while holding the first, or hold another filled trig to select a range.

For larger groups, use Shift → Sel. Trigs:

Control Selection
All Every stored trig; press again to clear the full selection.
Page Trigs on the visible page.
O:E Alternating odd or even grid positions; excludes off-grid trigs.
X/Y Every Xth stored trig in a cycle, counting trigs rather than empty steps.

X/Y cycles through 1/2, 2/2, 1/3, 3/3, 1/4, 4/4. A group selection focuses its first selected trig.

For a time-range selection, hold a trig in the piano roll and drag away from selected trigs to draw a horizontal selection range. Shift-tap adds a piano-roll trig; tapping one already selected toggles it.

Edit stored notes

Files and saving

Save Prism projects, reuse object presets and keep backups of external MIDI and user profiles.

Save a project

Project saving preserves Prism’s musical setup. It does not automatically save the receiving instrument or host session. Open Menu → Projects → Save Project, enter a session name and choose a folder. Standalone also provides a folder-menu shortcut.

Whole-session saving · standalone quick folder menu or Menu → Projects

Projects save the session’s musical state; global device settings remain separate. A project save does not guarantee that external Staging MIDI files are embedded. Save the receiving instrument/host session too if it has its own sound or routing state.

Project save options
  1. Open the folder menu and choose Save, or open Menu → Projects → Save Project.
  2. For an untitled project, enter a session name and select a folder. Use New Folder if you need a new destination.
  3. Press SAVE SESSION. After a successful save, that named project is your current save destination.
  4. Use Save again to update it. Use Save As to create a separately identified project before making a variation.
Folders and project identity

Choose the default root or a named folder in the save form. New Folder creates and selects the folder; an empty folder name does nothing. An empty session name becomes Untitled with date/time.

Save preserves the loaded project’s identity. Save As gives the new file a separate identity. A save request is not proof of success; if the form remains open or an error appears, check the destination and retry before opening another project.

Open and manage saved projects

The project browser opens named Prism sessions and lets you manage the saved file collection.

  1. Open Menu → Projects → Open Project.
  2. Choose / [ Default ] for root-level projects or choose a named folder.
  3. In the project list, identify the file by name and last-modified date/time. Use the sort control for alphabetical order or most recently modified first.
  4. Tap the project row to load it. Successful loading closes the menu, updates Recent Projects and makes it the current Save destination.
Project browser

The header names the current folder; Back returns to folder selection. Reopening remembers the last folder/list view. This browser loads directly from a row; it does not use the quick menu’s Recent confirmation. Save edits you need before loading a different project.

Recent tiles and deleting a file

In the quick folder popup, tap a Recent tile once to show Load?, then tap the same tile again within three seconds to load. Tapping a different tile changes the pending choice. Confirmation does not automatically save current work.

In the full Open Project browser, tap a row’s trash icon to reveal Delete and X. Press Delete to remove the file, or X to cancel. Confirmation expires after about three seconds; tapping the row during confirmation does not load it. Deleting also removes the Recent entry. Keep a separate backup if you need a way to recover a deleted saved file.

Start a new project

Save your work before choosing New Project; Prism asks you to confirm before discarding the current project.

In the quick folder menu, press New Project, then Discard changes? within three seconds. The drawer route Menu → Projects → New Project also confirms twice, with a two-second window.

New Project confirmation

These confirmations appear even if the project has no unsaved edits; they do not save first. A new project clears the current project and its Ableton pairing intent. It does not delete saved project files or presets.

Recover an earlier snapshot

Recover Autosave restores an earlier saved snapshot; this may replace several recent edits at once. Open Menu → Projects → Recover Autosave to recall an earlier autosaved snapshot.

If it contains the work you need, save it under a deliberate project name or use Save As to keep the current saved version separate.

Autosave recovery

Menu → Preferences → Autosave Interval chooses 3, 5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 30 or 60 seconds and also controls undo-history spacing. Recovery operates on snapshots, so it is not a guarantee of one undo item per gesture. A snapshot predating an edit cannot recover that later edit. External Staging sources must still be available.

Reuse objects as presets

Object presets reuse a specific editor’s configuration, unlike a full project containing the broader song state.

Eligible object editor · dropdown arrow beside the top name/notification area

An object preset saves that kind of object, not the whole session. Project Save/Save As uses the folder/menu flow above.

Preset management
  1. Open the dropdown and check the category: for example Arp, Harmonizer, Hue, LFO, Sound, Track, Staging Palette or Chord Block.
  2. Tap + to open Name Preset and create a named object preset.
  3. For an object already identified with a saved preset, the save icon below + writes its current state back to that preset.
  4. Tap a preset name to load it. If its row edit controls are open, the first name tap closes those controls; tap the name again to load.
Rename, assign colors, filter and delete

Drum and CC profiles

Drum and CC/MIDI profiles are mapping resources: Drum profiles define pads and output-note identities; CC profiles define mapped controls for a receiving device. They use their own profile-library/editor access rather than the object dropdown’s generic save icon.

Load the required profile from the corresponding Drum or CC profile library before using its mapping. Automatically Rename Track can let a loaded profile rename a still-default track; a custom track name is retained. For backups, use the dedicated profile import/export access described below. A profile file does not contain exported sequence-note MIDI.

Back up and move your work

Projects, user profiles and external MIDI source folders can be different files; back up each source you rely on.

Back up the named Prism project and any related user profiles or MIDI source folders. These are separate resources: saving a project does not guarantee its external Staging MIDI files travel with it.

Backup locations

Drum-profile collection

  1. Open Menu → Projects → User Data → Import / Export Drum Profiles.
  2. Choose Export All and select the destination in the system folder picker. This copies the saved user collection, not only the active track’s profile; originals remain in Prism.
  3. To restore/add profiles, choose Import and select one or several profile files in the system file picker.
  4. Load an imported profile from the Drum profile library when you need it.

For CC/MIDI mappings, open the CC profile library. The profile backup page offers Export All and Import for mapping resources, rather than note sequences. Keep these resources with your project backup if your receiving setup depends on custom mappings. Import adds saved profiles for later selection; Keep the original backup when importing profiles.

Staging portability and export types

A Staging project saves bank settings, staging sequences and shared latch state, together with references to its source folders/files. Those external phrase files must remain available after reopening or moving the project. Keep the source MIDI folder hierarchy when copying your work; if slots are unavailable, reselect the source folder or reload their phrases in Staging.

Projects, object presets, Drum/CC mapping profiles, external MIDI phrases and host sessions are different resources. Preserve each resource your setup uses. The system file/folder pickers provide external file access; the Open Project browser described here does not establish a built-in project rename/move/share command.

MIDI import accepts note files. Profile export exports mapping definitions. Drum/CC profile export saves mappings, not sequence-note MIDI files. For audio recording, record the receiving instrument or host’s output.

Projects and host sessions

A Prism project and a containing AUv3 host session save different information. Both may be needed to reproduce a setup.

A named Prism project saves Prism’s musical state. An AUv3 host session saves the host’s instrument instances and routing; standalone and global preferences have different ownership.

Save the relevant document in each place when you need to restore the complete setup.

Host save scope

Save your AUv3 host session to preserve the Prism instance and the host’s instruments/routing. Reopening restores that instance through the host. This is separate from Prism project Save/Open, which gives you a named musical project inside Prism.

A project contains the musical session state; global device settings remain separate. Direct standalone project loading can restore saved standalone MIDI routing. Host restoration keeps the current standalone routes instead of replacing them with the archived ones. Older projects with no standalone route data retain current routes. Endpoint names being saved does not ensure disconnected hardware is available when reopened.

Troubleshooting

Find the symptom below and check its likely causes. Save a separate project copy before clearing or replacing material.

No sound, wrong instrument or doubled notes

If the playhead moves but you hear nothing, check filled trigs, MIDI routing, receiving channel and instrument audio output—in that order.

If notes double, look for two routes to the same receiver.

MIDI and audio checks

If the wrong instrument plays, inspect the destination and channel filter.

If notes double, look for both all-track and channel-sliced routes to the same receiver, or a host route sending the keyboard directly to the instrument as well as through Prism. See standalone routing and AUM setup.

Output and routing checks

  1. Confirm the correct track/pattern is selected and contains trigs. In AUv3, start the host’s transport and make sure the Prism node is active.
  2. Check the route from Prism’s MIDI output to the receiving instrument. A connected keyboard input alone does not provide an instrument output route.
  3. Match the receiver’s input-channel filter to the intended Prism track output. For Drums, also check each pad’s output note.
  4. Confirm the instrument has a sound loaded, its level is audible and its audio output reaches your hardware output/headphones.
  5. Check track/pad/lane mute, current Scene mute profile, Solo and conditional playback settings.

Mute, conditions and note-processing layers

Mutes, Chance/Every conditions, gates, Arp, routing and locks act at different layers. A stored trig does not guarantee a note reaches an instrument on every pass. Compare the active page/range, mute state and conditions before clearing anything.

For a note that keeps sounding, stop playback and inspect held/latching sources, Staging launches and the receiving instrument. Release the performance source and remove an unintended duplicate/feedback route. Hold Stop in Prism for Panic if notes remain stuck.

Incoming notes and controller feedback

Check both controller MIDI input and the separate LED-feedback return route.

Ableton integration

Ableton integration needs a working control-surface connection and a monitored receiving track.

For a missing Live connection, check PrismScript, both MIDI directions, the Ableton Controller switch, the Live track’s Monitor=In, then Hub → Resync.

For an unresponsive instrument or macro, inspect the Live track owner and mapping.

Live connection checks
  1. Confirm the complete current PrismScript package is installed and selected as a Live Control Surface.
  2. Check both MIDI directions and the selected Live surface input/output ports. Keep the host/device connection active.
  3. Confirm Ableton Controller is on in Prism and the target Live MIDI track has Monitor=In.
  4. Open Track Setup → Ableton Track. Check the intended tile’s owner, not only Live’s selection highlight. Wait for the request to finish.
  5. Use Hub → Resync to refresh labels, mapped values and sample waveform information.

If a send does nothing, verify the Live Set contains its return destination. If a macro does nothing, check its Live assignment. If a sample operation reports compatibility limits, follow that message rather than assuming the waveform means every edit is supported. An unsaved Live Set needs Live Save As before Prism’s Save Ableton command can save it.

Turn Menu → Controllers → Ableton Controller off to disable the overall integration. Hold Reset Track removes one track’s connection but also clears its sequence/setup; save first. The Mac MIDI Connector for controllers is independent of PrismScript and does not solve a missing Live control surface by itself.

Link Audio monitoring

Network audio requires a published Live channel and a matching Prism receive selection.

For Link Audio, confirm Live and Prism share a local network, Live publishes audio and Prism selects its Ableton Link → Audio → Receive channel.

If sound is delayed, try Auto latency before manual adjustment.

Link Audio checks

Standalone Link Audio

  • Use Live 12.4+ and put Live/Prism on the same local network.
  • Enable Link and Link Audio, allow requested local-network access, and publish Live’s output.
  • In Prism’s Ableton Link → Audio, select the intended Receive channel. No channels published indicates no stream is available.
  • Check the iPad’s active audio output/volume. A working MIDI connection or Link tempo display does not prove audio streaming is active.
  • For delayed or unstable monitoring, try Auto latency; with it off, adjust the 20–400 ms delay. Network quality affects the return path, so compare with local instrument monitoring before changing musical timing.

Link Audio is separate from Link timing and MIDI control. The documented Prism receive route is standalone; hosted AUv3 uses the host’s own audio setup. See Link Audio monitoring.

Capture destination and import actions can replace existing content. Save a project copy before a deliberate overwrite. A failed or empty capture is not proof that a hidden undo snapshot exists. Read the notification, verify destination/context, then retry the intended operation.

Recover Autosave uses earlier snapshots spaced by Autosave Interval, which also spaces undo history. It cannot guarantee every gesture or restore deleted external files. Support channels

Send Feedback, Roadmap & Feature Requests, Discord and Facebook open their respective destinations.

For a useful report, include the Prism version displayed in the menu, device/platform, standalone or AUv3 host name, the shortest steps, expected result and actual notification. For MIDI/controller problems, include the controller/model and input/output route; for Ableton, include Live version, PrismScript package and target Monitor setting. A screenshot of the affected context and a small saved project can help explain the issue. Send reports only through the chosen support destination; opening a menu item alone does not send one.
